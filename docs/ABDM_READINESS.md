# ABDM Readiness — SwasthyaSetu AI

**Bottom line: SwasthyaSetu AI is NOT integrated with ABDM**
(Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission), is **not** ABDM-certified, and claims **no
FHIR compliance**. This document explains what the prototype actually does, and
what real ABDM integration would require, so that nobody — in a demo, a pitch,
or a PHC — overstates it.

---

## 1. What exists today: a locally formatted ID, not an ABHA number

- `formatAbhaId(patientId)` in `src/lib/screening-engine.ts` takes the app's own
  patient ID (e.g. `PAT-…`), hashes it locally (an FNV-style hash), and formats
  the result as `91-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX` — 12 derived digits plus the `91` prefix.
- It is **deterministic and entirely local**: the same patient ID produces the
  same string on any device, online or offline, with no network call, no ABDM/
  NHA lookup, no OTP, no Aadhaar step, and no consent flow to any external
  system.
- Therefore it is an **"ABHA-style" display ID only**. It is not issued by the
  National Health Authority (NHA), it does not resolve in any ABDM system, it
  is not linked to the person's real ABHA number / ABHA address if they have
  one, and presenting it at another facility will not retrieve this patient's
  records from anywhere.
- The code and UI are honest about the shape of this if you read them
  ("ABHA-style Health ID" in `/admin`, "ABHA Health ID" on the slip) — but any
  verbal or written claim that the app "generates ABHA IDs" or "is ABDM-ready"
  would be false. Approved wording: **"a locally formatted, ABHA-style
  prototype ID."**

## 2. The printed referral slip is a prototype

- `components/PatientScreeningModal.tsx` renders an expandable slip headed
  **"Ayushman Bharat (ABDM) Prototype Digital Referral Slip"**, showing the
  local ABHA-style ID, patient ID, facility and priority, plus a decorative
  5×5 grid presented as a "Digital Referral QR Code".
- That grid is **not a real QR code**: it is generated by filling cells from
  character codes of the patient/screening IDs. It encodes no standard payload,
  no other system can scan it into a record lookup, and the slip text's promise
  of "instant longitudinal record lookup" at the PHC counter is aspirational
  prototype copy, not a working capability.
- The slip also defaults to a **simulated** facility ("Simulated PHC Rampur,
  Sitapur — Uttar Pradesh (Demo)") when no real referral exists, and referral
  records carry an `is_simulated` flag. Referrals are saved inside this app
  only; they are **not transmitted to any hospital/PHC system, ABDM gateway,
  or health-information exchange.**
- Keep the word **Prototype** on the slip until a real, certified integration
  replaces it. Do not print and hand this slip to a real patient as if it were
  an official ABDM document.

## 3. What real ABDM integration requires (M1 / M2 / M3)

ABDM integration is a programme run by the **National Health Authority (NHA)**,
not a library you install. In outline — always confirm the current requirements
on the official ABDM/NHA developer documentation before planning, because the
programme's specifics change:

| Milestone | What it generally covers |
|---|---|
| **M1 — ABHA integration** | Creating/verifying a real ABHA number and ABHA address for the patient through ABDM APIs, with the prescribed consent and authentication flows (e.g. OTP/Aadhaar-based flows as specified), linking it to the local patient record, and letting a patient find/link records via their ABHA address |
| **M2 — Health-record interoperability (HIP side)** | Acting as a Health Information Provider: building and sharing records as **FHIR** resources/bundles in the ABDM-specified profiles, responding to data requests from consent managers, implementing the ABDM **consent-manager** flow (no record moves without an approved digital consent artefact), encryption and data-push via the ABDM gateway |
| **M3 — Health-record consumption (HIU side)** | Acting as a Health Information User: requesting records from other providers through the consent manager, receiving and correctly rendering FHIR data received from other systems |

And around all three:

1. **Registration** of the organisation/system with ABDM/NHA as a participating
   partner, with the facility identifiers (e.g. the national facility registry
   identifiers for the PHCs) that the programme requires.
2. **Sandbox work first:** build and demonstrate every flow in the ABDM
   **sandbox** environment, with test ABHA accounts — never against production
   ABDM services during development.
3. **Certification / go-live approval by NHA:** pass the programme's
   integration testing and certification for each milestone before production
   access; production credentials and endpoints are issued through that
   process, not self-generated.
4. **Consent as a system feature, not a checkbox:** ABDM consent artefacts,
   their expiry/revocation handling, and audit of every data transfer are part
   of the integration — separate from, and additional to, this app's existing
   registration consent (see `docs/DATA_PROTECTION.md`).
5. **Security requirements** of the programme (encryption in transit and for
   the data-push payloads, key management, logging) — designed in, and
   evidenced during certification.

None of steps 1–5 has been started for SwasthyaSetu AI. There is no ABDM
registration, no sandbox integration, no certification, and no production
credential in this repo.

## 4. FHIR: no compliance claimed

- The app's data model is its own Postgres schema (`src/db/schema.ts`) with
  records serialised as application JSON/JSON-in-text columns. **It is not a
  FHIR model**: there are no FHIR `Patient` / `Observation` / `ServiceRequest`
  resources, no ABDM FHIR profiles, no FHIR terminology bindings, and no FHIR
  validation anywhere in the repo.
- If, in future work, an export is produced that is shaped to resemble FHIR
  (for mapping practice, a data-portability download, or sandbox preparation),
  it must be labelled exactly what it is: a **non-certified draft export**.
  "FHIR-shaped" is not "FHIR-compliant", and a draft export must never be
  described to a facility, funder, or auditor as interoperability.
- Real FHIR work belongs inside the M2/M3 programme in §3, against the
  ABDM-published profiles, and is only "compliant" when it has passed the
  relevant validation/certification — not when it parses.
